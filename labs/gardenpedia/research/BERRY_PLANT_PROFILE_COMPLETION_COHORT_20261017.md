# Berry Plant Profile completion cohort — 2026-10-17

## Scope and disposition

Individual editorial review completed for:

- American gooseberry — `Ribes hirtellum`
- Black Hawk black raspberry — `Rubus occidentalis`, cultivar Black Hawk
- American elderberry — `Sambucus canadensis`
- American cranberry — `Vaccinium macrocarpon`

All four are marked complete in `plant-profile-inventory-501-20261017.csv`.
The profiles retain explicit unknown compatibility dimensions rather than
inventing container size, system fit, or indoor-light values.

## Useful evidence and scope

- Kew POWO supports the accepted species identities; the two named/non-cultivar
  records remain distinct from cultivar-level claims.
- UMN Extension supplies Upper Midwest gooseberry/currant pruning, ripeness,
  use, powdery-mildew and regional Ribes-rule context. It is shared crop
  guidance, not a cultivar performance claim for `R. hirtellum`.
- UMN's raspberry guide lists Black Hawk as a vigorous, floricane-fruiting
  black raspberry with rich, sweet, firm fruit. Water, cane training, pruning,
  ripeness and repeat-picking guidance is labeled as general raspberry or
  black-raspberry guidance, not a Black Hawk trial. Disease resistance is not
  asserted.
- UMN and UF/IFAS use different nomenclatural contexts for American
  elderberry. The record retains Kew's accepted `S. canadensis` and notes the
  literature's `S. nigra subsp. canadensis` usage without treating all regional
  material as interchangeable.
- UMN's 2026 food-safety guidance corrected an unsafe overgeneralization:
  raw elderflowers, as well as raw berries and other plant parts, may pose a
  risk; dried elderflowers also require heat treatment. EN/ES Plant Profile
  and Harvest Use wording now reflect this, and the profile gives no flower
  preparation method.
- UMass and Penn State give different numeric cranberry soil-pH ranges
  (`<5` and `4–5.5`). The profile states the disagreement, emphasizes strongly
  acidic tested soil, and avoids presenting one cutoff as universal. Penn
  State's 2–3-foot spacing, average-moisture advice, dark-red hand-harvest cue,
  and approximate three-year first-fruit timing remain explicitly regional
  home-garden guidance.
- UMass says cranberry seed starting takes extreme patience and is not
  recommended; this is not rewritten as biological impossibility or as a
  seed-starting protocol.
