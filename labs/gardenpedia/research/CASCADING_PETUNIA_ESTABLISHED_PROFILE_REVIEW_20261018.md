# Cascading Petunia — established-plant profile review (2026-10-18)

## Evidence checked

- Kew POWO accepts *Petunia × atkinsiana* and lists *Petunia × hybrida* as a
  synonym. This taxonomy record does not identify the AeroGarden seed lot.
- The AeroGarden product page identifies a Cascading Petunias pod kit, describes
  pink/purple/white blooms, gives a product-listed height ceiling and flowering
  display claims, recommends trimming to manage canopy/light blockage, and gives
  general indoor-plant pest signs. These are product claims/instructions, not
  universal petunia facts or a named-cultivar specification.
- University of Minnesota Extension distinguishes petunia growth classes,
  recommends adequate outdoor sunlight and deadheading, says not to pinch
  spreading types, and describes occasional stem cutting for bouquets. Its
  sunlight, soil and container-watering advice is not converted into indoor
  AeroGarden settings.

## Editorial changes

- Clarified in EN/ES that the product seller does not provide a scientific name
  for its seed lot or a named cultivar; the catalog's *P. × atkinsiana* mapping
  is not represented as supplier-confirmed.
- Corrected the growth explanation so “cascading” is not conflated with UMN's
  separate horticultural classes or used to infer mature spread.
- Added product-scoped flowering timing/display claims, an explicit distinction
  between UMN deadheading/non-pinching guidance and AeroGarden trimming advice,
  practical ornamental bouquet cutting, and indoor-pest warning signs. The pest
  list is not presented as petunia-specific susceptibility.
- Kept the Plant/Seed boundary intact: no germination instructions were moved
  into the established-plant profile. No Harvest Use record was fabricated for
  an ornamental identity.
- Did not change the compatibility profile, seed record, or any nutrient-engine
  mapping. AeroGarden use remains conditional on the documented pod system;
  no general hydroponic target is inferred.

## Disposition

Status remains **partial**. The product-specific plant-care content is richer,
but the product source does not resolve the seed lot's taxonomic identity or
horticultural class. Retain that uncertainty rather than treating the accepted
Kew name as proof of the product's exact lot identity. The inventory still
reports 417 complete, 74 partial and 10 pending profiles (501 total).
