# Cover-crop established-plant review cohort — 2026-10-18

Reviewed the species-level profiles for common buckwheat, cereal rye, crimson
clover, oats, and white clover. The pass reuses the existing University of
Minnesota cover-crop evidence bundle rather than repeating broad searches.
Claims remain scoped to Minnesota/Upper Midwest field or landscape contexts;
field timing and rates are not home-container or hydroponic prescriptions.

## Evidence used

- University of Minnesota Extension, [Cover crop selection for vegetable growers](https://extension.umn.edu/agriculture/specialty-crops/vegetable-farming/cover-crop-selection-for-vegetable-growers) — buckwheat, crimson clover, oats, and winter rye uses and management.
- University of Minnesota Extension, [Getting started with cover crops](https://extension.umn.edu/agriculture/crop-production/cover-crops/getting-started-with-cover-crops) — regional cover-crop functions and field planting context.
- University of Minnesota Extension, [Spring management of cover crops](https://extension.umn.edu/agriculture/crop-production/cover-crops/spring-management-of-cover-crops) — termination and residue management context for winter cover crops.
- University of Minnesota Extension, [Dutch white clover](https://extension.umn.edu/garden-and-home/yard-and-garden/gardening-in-minnesota/yard-and-garden-problems/dutch-white-clover) — white-clover morphology, landscape conditions, flowering, spread, uses, and management.

## Profile decisions

- **Common buckwheat:** clarified the short cover-crop window, bloom value, rapid seed set, and termination before reseeding. No universal maturity date or cultivar claim was added.
- **Cereal rye:** made spring termination stage, residue thickness, and next-crop timing more actionable. These remain regional field practices, not small-garden equipment instructions.
- **Crimson clover:** separated mixture benefits from the risk that a pure stand may become weedy; sowing order remains an Upper Midwest crop-mix recommendation.
- **Oats:** added relative cover-crop strengths, quicker residue breakdown, pea-mixture use, and the source-specific ability to terminate without waiting for bloom. **Partial** remains appropriate because the broad species identity also represents grain and forage uses not covered by this evidence bundle.
- **White clover:** added species-level mature form and dimensions, outdoor conditions, bloom, living-mulch/pollinator uses, persistence, and small-patch management. The size range is not assigned to a named turf cultivar.

## Boundaries and disposition

No Seed Profile V0 exists for these five IDs; seed-stage protocol was not
duplicated into the Plant Profiles. Indoor light, hydroponic suitability, and
system spacing remain unknown. Four Plant Profiles pass their established
cover-crop/groundcover scope; oats remains partial for its broader grain/forage
identity. Harvest Use was not added because the reviewed identities are
represented for cover-crop or groundcover use, not as a particular food-crop
harvest selection.
