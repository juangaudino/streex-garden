# Cucurbit type and Harvest Use review — 2026-10-18

## Scope

Individually reviewed `bush-cucumber`, `cucumber-marketmore`,
`gherkin-cucumber`, `pickling-cucumber`, `pattypan-squash`, and `yellow-squash`.
Existing Plant guidance was preserved except for the demonstrated growth-form
scope correction below. Added bilingual Harvest Use records only; Seed Profiles
and propagation instructions were not changed.

## Evidence and editorial decisions

- UMN Extension's cucumber home-garden guide supports warm-season established
  care, moisture-retentive/drained soil, root-zone watering, fresh and pickled
  use, frequent harvest, and crop-level flowering/problem signals. These facts
  remain scoped to cucumber crop guidance; Minnesota field amounts and dates are
  not presented as universal or hydroponic prescriptions.
- UMN Extension's summer-squash guide explicitly includes pattypan, supports
  tender immature fruit, raw/cooked/grated use, edible blossoms, and frequent
  harvest. Its regional cultivation recommendations are not converted to a
  cultivar-specific footprint.
- The UMN summer-squash page still contains pressure-canning advice, but the
  National Center for Home Food Preservation says prior canning recommendations
  for summer squash/zucchini were withdrawn because safe process times could
  not be established; USU Extension likewise cautions against home-canning plain
  summer squash and describes freezing or tested pickled recipes. The profiles
  follow NCHFP/USU for preservation safety and do not reproduce the conflicting
  pressure-canning instruction.
- UMN and USU give different household refrigeration durations for summer
  squash. No single storage-life number is asserted. Cucumber guidance also
  varies with storage conditions, so the profile avoids a universal guarantee.
- General cucumber/summer-squash sources describe more than one growth form.
  `Marketmore`, `gherkin`, `pickling`, `pattypan`, and `yellow squash` entries
  do not identify a specific selection that resolves bush versus vining habit;
  their compatibility value is now `unknown`, with an explicit reason. The
  compact `bush-cucumber` identity retains its supported bush-type value.
- `gherkin-cucumber` remains `Cucumis sativus`, not *Cucumis anguria*. No exact
  gherkin size or universal pickling threshold is inferred. Pickling recipe
  proportions and process are left to tested NCHFP recipes.
