# Culinary-herb Harvest Use cohort — 2026-10-18

Reviewed and enriched the Harvest Use layer for Culantro, French Tarragon,
Russian Tarragon, and Shiso. These records already had bilingual, identity-
scoped established-plant profiles; work targeted the missing harvest/use layer
instead of rewriting those profiles.

## Editorial and evidence decisions

- **Culantro (*Eryngium foetidum*):** UF/IFAS supports taking older basal leaves
  while younger leaves continue growing, fine chopping because whole leaves
  can be tough, Puerto Rican sofrito use, and harvesting only what is needed
  because leaves do not store well. No storage duration or preservation method
  is asserted. Kew/UFIFAS identity remains species-level.
- **French Tarragon (*Artemisia dracunculus*, French horticultural type):**
  Illinois Extension supports young tips/leaves, fresh use, and vinegars, oils,
  marinades and salads; drying rapidly loses flavor. The entry preserves its
  distinction from Russian tarragon. French Tarragon is vegetatively propagated;
  no Seed Profile was added.
- **Russian Tarragon (*Artemisia dracunculus*, Russian horticultural type):**
  RHS distinguishes its grassy, slightly bitter flavor from the warmer anise
  character of French tarragon and supports regular tip harvest, fresh use,
  drying and freezing. The horticultural type was not promoted to a separate
  accepted species. General tarragon dish examples remain explicitly scoped as
  RHS crop/type guidance, not cultivar-specific flavor claims.
- **Shiso (*Perilla frutescens*, species scope):** NC State supports the plant
  identity and edible leaves; University of Vermont Extension supports raw,
  cooked or pickled use. The profile remains unspecified for green/purple form;
  no color-specific flavor, recipe or preservation protocol is claimed. The UVM
  item is tier 2 because it is an Extension news article authored by a Master
  Gardener intern, not a primary cultivar source.
- **French Sorrel (*Rumex scutatus*):** no additional Harvest Use record was
  added because its Plant Profile already communicates edible tart leaves,
  salad/cooking use and later-season bitterness. The NC State annual/perennial
  table-vs-narrative conflict remains visible; no storage duration or harvest
  interval was invented.

The new Harvest Use text is bilingual and uses existing public source IDs except
for one new scoped UVM source record. No private data, Seed Profile content,
compatibility mapping, Garden X runtime, or schema was changed in this cohort.

## Disposition

The four enriched identities are marked complete in the 501-identity editorial
inventory after review of identity scope, established-plant cultivation and
management, harvest/use, EN/ES alignment, and source references. Unknown
hydroponic or exact storage behaviors remain explicitly unknown; they are not
universal completion quotas for these outdoor/culinary profiles. Optional
companion and visual-guide records remain absent and were not treated as
mandatory. French Sorrel remains partial pending record-level reconciliation
of the source-internal lifecycle conflict.
