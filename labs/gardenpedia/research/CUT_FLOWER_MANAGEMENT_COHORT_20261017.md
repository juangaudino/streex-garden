# Cut-flower management cohort — 2026-10-17

## Scope

Reviewed three existing identities only: `stock-matthiola-incana`,
`sweet-scabiosa-scabiosa-atropurpurea`, and `snapdragon-antirrhinum-majus`.
The edits enrich established-plant care and harvest behavior. Existing Seed
Profiles and compatibility values were not changed. Outdoor soil/light evidence
was not converted into hydroponic or indoor grow-light claims.

## Evidence and editorial decisions

- **Stock / Matthiola incana:** NC State supports cool-season growth, fragrant
  flowers, temperature-sensitive performance, moisture/drainage, bloom context,
  and a white-mold caveat. Mississippi State Extension's farmer-florist guide
  supports cutting at one-third to one-half of florets open. That harvest stage
  is labeled cut-flower production guidance, not cultivar vase-life or rebloom
  evidence. Commercial interspecific hybrids are not treated as the species or
  as a named cultivar.
- **Sweet Scabiosa / Scabiosa atropurpurea:** NC State supports regional annual
  versus mild-winter persistence, full sun, alkaline organic-rich soil,
  deadheading tradeoffs, bloom timing, possible staking, wet-soil/root-rot risk,
  minor aphids/mites, and self-seeding/invasive status only in some countries.
  No general cut stage is documented. A cut stage from a named QIS cultivar was
  deliberately not generalized to this unnamed species identity; the inventory
  remains partial for that unresolved practical detail.
- **Common Snapdragon / Antirrhinum majus:** NC State supports species-level
  flowering, cultivar-dependent colors and stature, low drought tolerance,
  staking of tall forms, cut-flower use, bitter edible flowers as garnish, and
  fungal disease concerns. Mississippi State Extension supports harvest when
  the lower third to half of the spike is open, labeled as production guidance;
  cultivar-specific postharvest behavior and vase life were not generalized.
  The species-level edible-flower note is represented in Harvest Use and is not
  a blanket safety claim for unidentified ornamental or florist flowers.
