# Established-plant herb review cohort — 2026-10-18

Reviewed: French Sorrel (*Rumex scutatus*), Garden Angelica (*Angelica archangelica*), Hyssop (*Hyssopus officinalis*), and Society Garlic (*Tulbaghia violacea*).

## Evidence and editorial decisions

- **French Sorrel:** POWO accepts *Rumex scutatus* as a perennial. NC State's page narrative also describes a creeping perennial, but its structured trait table labels it annual. The Plant Profile preserves this source-internal conflict rather than silently erasing it. NC State supports outdoor sun-to-partial-shade and well-drained medium-moisture context, edible tart leaves, and later-season bitterness; the source does not set a harvest start date, cutting interval, or storage duration. Hydroponic fit remains unknown.
- **Garden Angelica:** NC State describes a robust biennial or short-lived perennial and a first-year foliage/second-year flowering pattern. Its size table and prose differ on maximum flowering-stem height; the profile identifies both as source-reported garden-scale observations rather than a container guarantee. Culinary parts are listed, but no part-specific harvest timing/technique is assigned. Toxic look-alike and photosensitivity/contact-dermatitis cautions remain prominent.
- **Hyssop:** NC State evidence is scoped to *Hyssopus officinalis*, explicitly distinguished from *Agastache* species also called hyssop. Corrected the V1 compatibility growth/light provenance so it no longer cites unrelated herb/water-crop sources. Full sun to partial shade is represented only as outdoor exposure; it does not create an indoor grow-light classification. Spring trimming, summer flowering, edible leaves/tips/flowers, and possible nematodes are included without an unsupported deadheading or harvest-calendar rule.
- **Society Garlic:** NC State supports species-level clumping/arching habit, edible leaves/flowers, flowering from early summer into fall, and possible slug/snail foliage damage. The profile does not equate *Tulbaghia* with garlic chives or true garlic, infer seed propagation from bulbous growth, or claim hydroponic suitability.
