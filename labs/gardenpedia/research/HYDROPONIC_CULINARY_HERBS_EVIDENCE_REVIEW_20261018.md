# Hydroponic culinary-herb evidence review — 2026-10-18

## Editorial decision

Added research-context sections to the public Plant Profiles for `common-chives`
and `italian-giant-parsley`, following the direct *Allium tuberosum* study
reviewed in `GARLIC_CHIVES_HYDROPONIC_EVIDENCE_REVIEW_20261018.md`. These sources
show that the relevant species have been studied in hydroponic production; they
do not prove fit for every countertop machine or justify a Garden nutrient
engine mapping. No compatibility profile or private seed ownership record was
changed. Both identities remain `partial` for their unresolved product/system
questions.

## Common chives — *Allium schoenoprasum*

The American Society for Horticultural Science conference abstract reports a
University of Tennessee experiment growing common chives in nutrient-film
hydroponics under commercial greenhouse conditions. Supplemental HPS or LED
light increased shoot fresh and dry mass relative to the natural-sunlight
control in that experiment. The authors state that further work is needed to
establish precise light quantity and quality. Accordingly, the profile describes
the study context and does not infer a home grow-light tier or appliance fit.

## Italian Giant parsley — *Petroselinum crispum*

A 2022 primary study reports hydroponic cultivation of *P. crispum* and
comparison of Hoagland and Thakur nutrient solutions, with stronger
morphological/biomass results under the study's Hoagland treatment. This is
species-level evidence. It does not resolve the catalog's Italian Giant / Giant
of Italy selection-name question, establish cultivar-specific dimensions, or
authorize copying the experimental formula into the nutrient engine.

## Chervil — *Anthriscus cerefolium*

Stapleton and Hochmuth's 2000–01 UF/IFAS greenhouse trial grew chervil in
Verti-Gro vertical towers filled with perlite, using one tower of 32 plants for
each herb. Chervil's first harvest was reported 21 days after transplanting;
weekly hand harvests were made at the petiole base after petioles exceeded
6–8 inches, and bolting or severe pest pressure triggered replanting when
marketable quality was lost. These are Florida trial details, not a home
calendar or pod count. The same study separately listed Italian (flat-leaf)
parsley, making it more directly relevant to that catalog type than generic
species hydroponic evidence alone.
