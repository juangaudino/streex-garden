# Leafy Greens Harvest Use Cohort — 2026-10-17

## Scope

Added reviewed bilingual Harvest Use records for six existing identities:

- Baby Spinach (`baby-spinach`)
- Collard Greens (`collard-greens`)
- Corn Salad / mâche (`corn-salad-valeriana-locusta`)
- Endive (`endive`)
- Escarole (`escarole`)
- Celtuce (`celtuce-lactuca-sativa-augustana`)

This is a targeted content cohort, not a completion sign-off for these six full Plant Profiles. Inventory statuses remain partial or pending until the remaining profile and auxiliary evidence is individually reviewed.

## Evidence and scope decisions

- **Baby spinach:** USU's agronomy source covers spinach generally, not a named baby-leaf cultivar. A separate USU nutrition guide supports the culinary examples. No baby-leaf harvest size or maturity date was invented.
- **Collard greens:** UF/IFAS supports young-leaf culinary use, washing, successive outer-leaf harvest and a Florida crop-pest list. Florida dates and cultivar-specific traits were not generalized. The Plant Profile's previously unresolved pest section now records only the source's listed pests and advises confirming identification before intervention.
- **Corn salad:** USU supports the mild, nutty salad use, leaf-versus-whole-plant harvest choices, and approximate leaf/plant cues. The profile keeps POWO's accepted `Valeriana locusta` separate from the Extension source's `Valerianella locusta` synonym usage.
- **Endive:** UMN supports salad and cooking uses, cool-season crop context, shallow-root moisture, leaf/whole-plant harvest and heat/long-day bolting. Minnesota dates and field spacing are not carried into other climates or indoor systems. The record excludes Belgian endive (witloof).
- **Escarole:** UMN distinguishes broad, smooth-leaved escarole within `Cichorium endivia` and notes that it is usually cooked. This remains distinct from the general Endive identity; no cultivar or cooking time is asserted.
- **Celtuce:** UC Davis documents the stem as the primary edible part, leaves as also edible, and raw/cooked stem use. No stem diameter, cultivar, recipe or preparation time was inferred.
