# Lemon thyme established-plant review — 2026-10-18

The existing `lemon-thyme` Plant Profile was reviewed against the established-
plant rubric and enriched in EN/ES. The Seed Profile was not changed.

## Identity and taxonomy disposition

- POWO/WCVP accepts *Thymus × citriodorus* as the hybrid *T. pulegioides* ×
  *T. vulgaris*. Iowa State Extension also identifies lemon thyme by this
  hybrid name; the NC State page title and structured taxon fields agree.
- NC State's narrative contains a conflicting sentence calling it a distinct
  species, and lists *Thymus citriodorus* as a prior name. The record now follows
  the accepted POWO hybrid name, records the former name as a source-scoped
  synonym, and preserves the narrative conflict in taxonomy provenance.
- No named cultivar is assigned. The profile remains a lemon-thyme group/taxon
  record, not a cultivar claim.

## Content and evidence decisions

- NC State supports an evergreen mounding subshrub, about 6–12 in tall and up
  to about 1 ft wide, aromatic edible leaves, pink mid-summer flowers,
  full-to-partial sun, well-drained soil and root rot in overly moist soil.
- NC State's structured height row (“6 ft. 0 in. - 1 ft. 0 in.”) is malformed
  and excluded. The clear narrative height is rounded to 15–30 cm; its width
  is represented as an outdoor maximum of about 30 cm. Neither is a cultivar or
  indoor-system guarantee.
- Iowa State Extension provides genus-level thyme guidance for light drainage,
  light pruning after flowering, seasonal harvest and best-flavor timing near
  first bloom. Its page explicitly names lemon thyme as *T. × citriodorus*.
- Iowa State and NC State problem notes support moisture/root-rot caution;
  occasional spider mites in hot, dry weather and aphids are retained as
  general thyme risks, not identity-specific susceptibility claims.
- Hydroponic suitability, nutrient targets, indoor-light tier and system
  spacing remain unknown. Outdoor sun exposure is not mapped to grow-light
  intensity.
- Harvest Use remains coordinated with the profile: edible leaves/flowers and
  culinary use are supported, while no cultivar-specific flavor, storage life
  or preservation guarantee is added.
