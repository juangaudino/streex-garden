## Scope

## Claims and evidence

- **Cantaloupe — `Cucumis melo`, cantaloupe type, no named cultivar.** Iowa State
  Extension documents common-name overlap between cantaloupe and muskmelon and
  crop-level fresh preparation. Its food-use guide supports washing/scrubbing the
  rind before cutting, wedges, fruit kebabs with yogurt, salsa, and airtight
  refrigeration of cut melon for up to five days. The existing USU source
  continues to support this profile's separate crop-level vine, pollination,
  moisture, harvest-cue, and problem claims.
- **Honeydew — `Cucumis melo`, cultivated inodorus group, no named cultivar.**
  Iowa State Extension states honeydew does not slip from the vine at maturity;
  color, blossom-end softness, light aroma, and retaining about two inches of
  stem are the relevant crop-level cues. The food-use source supports rind
  washing, fresh serving, and the same cut-melon household handling. These
  facts are not transferred to cantaloupe maturity behavior.
- **Sugar Pumpkin — `Cucurbita pepo`, pie-pumpkin type, no named cultivar.**
  Illinois Extension supports cooking mature pumpkin flesh for pie, soup, or
  bread; washing before cutting; piercing and cooking whole to ease cutting;
  freezing cooked pulp; and the food-safety boundary against home-canning purée
  or pumpkin butter. Safe canning is limited to pumpkin cubes processed with a
  current tested pressure-canning procedure. The guidance is pumpkin-level and
  is not represented as Sugar Pumpkin-specific performance.
