# Ocimum Species Harvest Use Cohort — 2026-10-17

## Scope

Reviewed the existing species-level identities `basil-ocimum-basilicum`
(*Ocimum basilicum*, cultivar unspecified) and
`tulsi-holy-basil-ocimum-tenuiflorum` (*O. tenuiflorum*, cultivar unspecified).
They share a genus but are distinct species and retain separate sources and
culinary descriptions. The basil record does not inherit Genovese, Sweet, Thai,
or other cultivar/type traits; tulsi is not represented as an *O. basilicum*
cultivar.

## Editorial changes

- Added bilingual Harvest Use for common *O. basilicum* basil: fresh use shortly
  before serving, USU's stem-in-water handling, its stated 7–10 day covered
  refrigerator method, shade drying, and freezing trade-offs. These are species-
  level crop guidelines and not a cultivar-specific storage guarantee.
- Added bilingual Harvest Use for tulsi: NC State describes the aromatic leaves
  as commonly cooked or used in teas rather than raw, with a spicy clove/mint/
  basil flavor. No medicinal effects or dosage are represented.
- Added a sourced, bilingual growth section for tulsi: aromatic annual or
  short-lived perennial/subshrub, erect and multi-stemmed, rapid growth, and a
  source-reported height around 3 feet. This is species-level description, not a
  cultivar measurement.

## Evidence and caveat

- [Utah State University Extension — How to Grow Basil in Your Garden](https://extension.usu.edu/yardandgarden/research/basil-in-the-garden)
  documents common basil harvest and food-use/storage methods. Its flower-
  pinching statements are internally inconsistent: one passage says leaf
  removal before flowering promotes more foliage, while its FAQ says pinching
  flowers does not stimulate new foliage. This cohort does not repeat a claim
  that pinching flowers itself increases leaf production.
- [NC State Extension — Holy Basil](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/ocimum-tenuiflorum/)
  documents the species' growth, edible aromatic leaves, cooked/tea use, and
  distinguishes it from culinary basil. The ethnobotany text is not converted to
  a therapeutic or health recommendation.

## Disposition

Both profiles remain **partial** because hydroponic suitability is not
established. Unknown cultivar-dependent size/habit for unspecified *O.
basilicum* also remains explicit. No nutrient-engine mapping or unsupported
indoor prescription was added.
