## Sweet pea — *Lathyrus odoratus* (species/type)

- Identity, annual life cycle, bush/climbing forms, species-level size ranges,
  cool-weather preference, full sun, fertile well-drained soil, even moisture,
  support, deadheading, heat/humidity concern, and possible slugs/snails/aphids
  are based on NC State Extension's species page.
- RHS supplies garden-level support, watering, deadheading and optional pinching
  detail. Cornell's Zone 5 high-tunnel source is used only to identify this as a
  cut-flower crop; its production schedule is not copied into a home calendar.
- Illinois Extension supports continued cutting for flowering. NC State and UC
  ANR warn against eating sweet-pea pods / confusing the ornamental with edible
  *Pisum sativum*.
- No named cultivar, exact system compatibility, hydroponic recipe or single
  mature-size endpoint is asserted. Growth habit records both bush/mounded and
  climbing forms. Seed/Profile boundary is preserved: this Plant Profile
  describes the established ornamental plant and does not add germination facts.

## Pacific Beauty calendula — *Calendula officinalis* 'Pacific Beauty'

- RHS provides named-selection identity, color, broad height/spread range,
  outdoor position and possible pests/diseases. The page's reported 0.5–1 m
  height differs from UNH Extension's 10–12 in general-species description;
  the profile retains the RHS cultivar-scoped range and explicitly records the
  scope/context discrepancy rather than averaging the figures.
- RHS and Extension/NC State support outdoor sun/partial-shade, soil/moisture,
  pinching, deadheading and possible problem guidance. Soil/field guidance is
  not mapped to hydroponics or indoor-light tiers.
- UNH Extension supports half-open cut-flower harvest for longer vase life and
  fresh/dried petal use. Harvest Use methods explicitly keep those culinary
  claims at *Calendula officinalis* species scope, not a unique Pacific Beauty
  flavor or medicinal claim.
- No Garden X private data, seed-packet observations, or system performance are
  used. Unknown hydroponic fit remains unknown.
